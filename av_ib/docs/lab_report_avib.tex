\section{Motivation}

Large audio-visual language models tend to produce confident responses even
when a required modality is absent or corrupted.
This \emph{hallucination under modal degradation} is the focus of recent
training-free mitigation work: MAD~\cite{mad2026} addresses it through
modality-aware contrastive decoding on AVHBench, while DPA~\cite{dpa2026}
perturbs attention queries to reduce cross-modal spurious dependencies.
Both methods intervene at inference time without modifying model weights.

We take a complementary \emph{trained} approach: compress the video channel
through a Variational Information Bottleneck~\cite{alemi2017vib} so that the
model is structurally uncertain about the video stream when it carries
corrupted or uninformative content, then teach the model to \emph{abstain}
under that uncertainty via Direct Preference Optimization~\cite{rafailov2023dpo}.

\section{Background}

\subsection{Variational Information Bottleneck}

Alemi et al.~\cite{alemi2017vib} formulate representation learning as a
rate-distortion problem.  Given input $X$ and label $Y$, a stochastic encoder
$q_\phi(Z|X)$ is trained to compress $X$ into $Z$ while retaining task-relevant
information:
\[
  \min_\phi \;\underbrace{I(Z;X)}_{\text{rate}} - \beta\,\underbrace{I(Z;Y)}_{\text{distortion}^{-1}}
\]
Under a diagonal Gaussian encoder $q_\phi(Z|X) = \mathcal{N}(\mu_\phi(X),
\operatorname{diag}(\sigma^2_\phi(X)))$ and a standard Gaussian prior $p(Z)
= \mathcal{N}(0,I)$, the rate term is the closed-form KL divergence:
\[
  D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_\phi(Z|X)\,\|\,p(Z))
  = \frac{1}{2}\sum_d \left(\mu_d^2 + \sigma_d^2 - \log\sigma_d^2 - 1\right)
\]
and the distortion is minimised by maximising the log-likelihood of $Y$ given
$Z$.  The combined objective is:
\[
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{VIB}} = -\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(Z|X)}\bigl[\log p_\theta(Y|Z)\bigr]
    + \beta\,D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(q_\phi(Z|X)\,\|\,\mathcal{N}(0,I)\right)
\]
At inference, noise is turned off ($z = \mu_\phi(X)$), and the magnitude of
the KL term at test time serves as a \emph{confidence proxy}: a large
posterior-to-prior gap means the video carried information; a small gap means
it was uninformative or corrupted.

\subsection{Direct Preference Optimization}

Rafailov et al.~\cite{rafailov2023dpo} show that RLHF with a KL-constrained
reward is equivalent to directly optimising a binary cross-entropy over
preference pairs, bypassing explicit reward modelling:
\[
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DPO}} = -\mathbb{E}_{(x,y_w,y_l)}\!\left[
    \log\sigma\!\left(
      \beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_w|x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_w|x)}
    - \beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_l|x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_l|x)}
    \right)\right]
\]
where $y_w$ is the preferred (chosen) response, $y_l$ the dispreferred
(rejected) response, and $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is the frozen reference policy.
We use DPO to steer the bottlenecked model toward abstention on corrupted
inputs without designing an explicit reward function.

\section{Architecture}

\subsection{Backbone: Qwen3-Omni-30B}

The backbone is \textbf{Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Instruct}~\cite{qwen3omni2025},
a 30.2B-parameter Mixture-of-Experts multimodal language model with three
sub-modules relevant here:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Audio tower} (648M parameters): encodes the audio stream to
        token sequences of dimension 2048.
  \item \textbf{Visual encoder} (539M parameters): encodes video frames to
        token sequences of dimension 2048.
  \item \textbf{Thinker text model} (30.2B, MoE): a transformer decoder with
        hidden size 2048 that attends over the interleaved audio/video/text
        token sequence and generates the response.
\end{itemize}
All backbone components are kept \textbf{frozen}.  Low-rank adapters (LoRA,
$r{=}16$, $\alpha{=}16$) are optionally added to the thinker's attention and
MLP projections (\texttt{q\_proj}, \texttt{k\_proj}, \texttt{v\_proj},
\texttt{o\_proj}, \texttt{linear\_fc1}, \texttt{linear\_fc2}); these account
for $\approx 0.7$M trainable parameters out of 30B.

\subsection{Token Injection via CMIBSplicer}

A hook-based \texttt{CMIBSplicer} sits between the encoders and the thinker.
It:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Captures the encoder outputs ($z_a \in \mathbb{R}^{N_a \times 2048}$,
        $z_v \in \mathbb{R}^{N_v \times 2048}$) and the placeholder masks that
        locate audio/video token slots in the thinker's embedding matrix.
  \item Passes the captured tensors to a \emph{provider} (the bottleneck
        pipeline described below).
  \item Writes the bottlenecked tokens back into the thinker's
        \texttt{inputs\_embeds} via \texttt{masked\_scatter}, leaving text
        tokens untouched.
\end{enumerate}
This design requires zero modifications to the frozen backbone's forward pass.

\subsection{Sink-Aware Video Bottleneck (\texttt{SinkAwareVIB})}

Not all token positions carry the same information.  Inspection of the visual
encoder reveals two ``attention sink'' dimensions (indices 985 and 1992) that
consistently receive anomalously large activation magnitudes and dominate the
KL.  Applying a uniform KL pressure to these dimensions suppresses them
without genuine compression, destabilising training.

The \texttt{SinkAwareVIB} classifies each token as a \emph{sink} or
\emph{non-sink} before applying the bottleneck:
\[
  \phi_t = \max\!\left(\,|x_{t,985}|,\;|x_{t,1992}|\,\right),
  \qquad
  \text{sink}(t) = \mathbf{1}\!\left[\phi_t \geq \tau\right], \quad \tau = 18.0
\]
where $x_t$ is the RMS-normalised token vector.  Two independent residual
bottleneck paths are applied (both zero-initialised so $z = x$ at step 0):
\begin{align*}
  \mu(x) &= x + W_\mu\,x, \\
  \log\sigma^2(x) &= W_{\log\sigma}\,x\;\;\text{(clamped to }[-10,10]\text{)}.
\end{align*}
The KL penalty is then split by sink classification:
\[
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KL}} =
    \underbrace{\sum_{t:\,\neg\text{sink}} \mathrm{KL}_t}_{\text{non-sink tokens}}
    + \;\alpha_s \cdot \underbrace{\sum_{t:\,\text{sink}} \mathrm{KL}_t}_{\text{sink tokens}},
  \qquad \alpha_s = 0.01
\]
Sink tokens thus receive $100\times$ lower KL pressure, allowing them to pass
through essentially unchanged while the non-sink tokens are genuinely
compressed.

\subsection{Audio Bottleneck (\texttt{NormTopKSinkVIB} or identity)}

The audio encoder has near-zero correlation ($\rho \approx -0.06$) with the
video sink dimensions, so the dimension-based sink classifier is invalid for
audio.  When an audio bottleneck is used (AV bottleneck variant), sink
classification defaults to the top-40\% tokens by L2 norm:
\[
  \text{sink}(t) = \mathbf{1}\!\left[\|x_t\|_2 \geq \kappa_{0.6}(\{x\})\right]
\]
where $\kappa_{0.6}$ is the 60th-percentile norm threshold.  The
KL split and residual paths are otherwise identical to the video bottleneck.
In the \emph{video-only} variant the audio path is a pure identity (no
bottleneck, no KL).

\subsection{Optional Cross-Modal Fusion (\texttt{MutualCrossAttention})}

After the per-modal bottlenecks, an optional symmetric cross-attention fusion
module updates each modality's tokens using the other as context:
\[
  z_v' = z_v + \mathrm{Attn}(z_v,\,z_a),
  \qquad
  z_a' = z_a + \mathrm{Attn}(z_a,\,z_v)
\]
with pre-norm and a residual FFN, all zero-initialised (identity at step 0).
The fusion uses $H{=}8$ attention heads and one block.  It is disabled in the
\texttt{nofusion} variants used in these experiments.

\subsection{Training Objective}

The full training loss is:
\[
  \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{NLL}}
    + \beta_v\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KL}}^v
    + \beta_a\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KL}}^a
    + \lambda_{\mathrm{aux}}\left(
        \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{aux}}^v + \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{aux}}^a
      \right)
\]
where $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{NLL}}$ is the teacher-forced NLL on the answer
span, $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KL}}^{v/a}$ are the bottleneck KL terms
(with sink weighting), and $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{aux}}^{v/a}$ are auxiliary
cross-entropy losses from linear heads on the video/audio bottleneck outputs
(weight $\lambda_{\mathrm{aux}}{=}0.1$).  Training uses AdamW
(lr$\,{=}10^{-4}$, weight decay $0.05$, gradient clip $1.0$).

\subsection{Abstention Training}

After bottleneck pre-training, a DPO stage teaches the model when to abstain.
Preference pairs are mined from the training set: for each clean clip where the
model answers correctly, two corrupted variants are generated
(\texttt{vid\_zero}: all frames zeroed; \texttt{vid\_mean}: frames replaced by
channel mean).  The chosen response is the abstain string
(``I can't tell''); the rejected response is the model's overconfident answer
under corruption.

At evaluation, abstention is detected by teacher-forcing the NLL of each real
answer option (A/B/C/D for AVQA; yes/no for AVHBench) plus the abstain string,
and picking the minimum-NLL continuation.

\section{Benchmarks}

\textbf{AVQA}~\cite{yang2022avqa}: 57,015 video clips from
VGGSound~\cite{vggsound2020}, 4-way multiple-choice (letters A--D).
Questions are categorised as View / Sound / Both by the annotation.
Because VGGSound is curated around sound events, these questions are
predominantly answerable from audio alone, making it a weak substrate for
the video-abstention experiment.

\textbf{AVHBench}~\cite{avhbench2024}: open-domain binary yes/no questions
over VGGSound and AudioCaps clips, covering three task types --- AV Matching,
Audio-driven Video Hallucination, and Video-driven Audio Hallucination.
Both modalities are genuinely required for these judgment tasks, which is why
this benchmark is the primary test of the fail-safe abstention mechanism.

\section{Results on AVQA}

\subsection{Accuracy}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
\toprule
\textbf{Model} & \textbf{Acc (\%)} & \textbf{Mean NLL} & \textbf{Mean KL (video)} & $n$ \\
\midrule
Untrained baseline     & \textbf{94.0} & 0.112 & 2904.9 & 300 \\
Video bottleneck base  & 93.3          & 0.105 & \textbf{1601.4} & 300 \\
LoRA baseline          & 91.7          & 0.125 & 2904.9 & 300 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The bottleneck halved the video KL (2905 $\to$ 1601) at negligible accuracy
cost ($\Delta{=}{-}0.7$ pp on $n{=}300$).  LoRA fine-tuning slightly degraded
accuracy, consistent with the base model being near ceiling on this
audio-dominated sample.

\subsection{Risk-Coverage (Abstention)}

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{llrrr}
\toprule
\textbf{Model} & \textbf{Corruption} & \textbf{Coverage} & \textbf{Risk} & \textbf{Group} \\
\midrule
Video bottleneck DPO & identity   & 100.0\% &  8.7\% & train \\
                     & vid\_zero  &  99.3\% &  8.1\% & train \\
                     & vid\_mean  &  98.0\% & 11.6\% & train \\
                     & vid\_noise & 100.0\% &  8.7\% & held-out \\
                     & vid\_scale & 100.0\% &  6.0\% & held-out \\
\midrule
AV bottleneck DPO    & identity   & 100.0\% & 11.3\% & train \\
                     & vid\_zero  &  93.3\% &  8.6\% & train \\
                     & vid\_mean  &  86.7\% & 16.9\% & train \\
                     & vid\_noise &  99.3\% & 10.1\% & held-out \\
                     & vid\_scale & 100.0\% &  8.0\% & held-out \\
\midrule
Video bottleneck SFT & identity   & 100.0\% &  8.7\% & train \\
                     & vid\_mean  &  88.0\% &  9.8\% & train \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Abstention did not emerge on AVQA: coverage remains near 100\% and risk stays
at $8$--$17$\% regardless of corruption.  The likely cause is an ecological
mismatch: VGGSound questions are largely answerable from audio alone, so
zeroing the video does not increase the error rate.  A model that is already
correct without video has no incentive to abstain when video is corrupted.

\section{Ongoing: AVHBench Evaluation}

The same five checkpoints are being evaluated on AVHBench~\cite{avhbench2024}.
Because both modalities are genuinely required for AVHBench's judgment tasks,
we expect: (i) risk to rise under \texttt{vid\_zero}/\texttt{vid\_mean};
(ii) the DPO models to abstain (coverage drops) while the baselines maintain
coverage with elevated risk; (iii) audio corruption
(\texttt{aud\_zero}/\texttt{aud\_mean}) to also affect coverage for
audio-driven tasks, unlike the AVQA case where audio corruption was a
true negative control.
