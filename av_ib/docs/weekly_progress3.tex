\section{This week}

\begin{frame}{What I did this week}

\begin{block}{SFT vs DPO risk--coverage experiment: results in}
  SFT over-abstained catastrophically (coverage\,=\,0 on \emph{every} condition,
  including clean). DPO learned the right selective-abstention pattern: nearly full
  coverage on clean and audio-only corruptions, near-zero coverage on trained
  video corruptions (\texttt{vid\_zero}, \texttt{vid\_mean}).
\end{block}

\begin{block}{DPO generalises partially to held-out corruptions}
  \texttt{vid\_scale} (unseen at training): coverage drops to 0.7--10.7\,\% ---
  good generalisation. \texttt{vid\_noise}: coverage stays high (94\,\%) ---
  the model cannot recognise white-noise corruption as ``missing evidence.''
  Negative control (audio zeroed): coverage stays at 99\,\% as intended.
\end{block}

\begin{block}{Attention analysis (Axis 2): where the model actually looks}
  Gradient $\times$ input saliency across 10 AVHBench clips. Video dominates by
  token count (17:1), not by per-token weight. The VIB concentrates attention
  on the sound source: entropy $0.78\!\to\!0.69$, top-5\% mass $0.53\!\to\!0.74$.
\end{block}

\end{frame}

\bottomrule

\end{block}

\end{column}

\begin{column}{0.47\textwidth}

\begin{block}{Reading the table}
{\footnotesize
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{3pt}
  \item \textbf{SFT}: completely collapsed. After maximising NLL of
        ``I can't tell,'' the model \emph{only} says that --- on
        clean clips too. No coverage anywhere.
  \item \textbf{DPO}: the KL leash to $\pi_\text{ref}$ prevented
        collapse. Clean coverage stays at 99.3\,\%; trained
        corruptions drop to 0\,\% --- perfect on those.
  \item \textbf{Held-out generalisation}: \texttt{vid\_scale}
        transfers well (near-zero coverage). \texttt{vid\_noise}
        does not --- model sees noise as signal.
  \item \textbf{Negative control}: coverage is preserved when only
        the inert (audio) modality is corrupted --- the model
        correctly does not abstain.
\end{itemize}}
\end{block}

\begin{alertblock}{Why SFT fails here}
{\footnotesize
$\mathcal L_\text{SFT}$ has no leash: if the loss is minimised by
always predicting ``cant,'' it will. DPO's margin loss
\emph{requires} the model to prefer the correct response on positive
examples, preventing this collapse.}
\end{alertblock}

\end{column}
\end{columns}

\section{Attention analysis}

\begin{frame}{Where does the model put its attention? (Axis 2)}

\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.55\textwidth}

\begin{block}{Gradient $\times$ input saliency (10 AVHBench clips)}
{\footnotesize
We hook the first decoder layer and backpropagate through $-\log p(\hat y)$:
\[ s_i = \mathrm{ReLU}\!\Big(\nabla_{x_i}\log p(\hat y)\cdot x_i\Big) \]
This gives a clean, causal signal immune to attention sinks.}
\end{block}

\begin{block}{Per-modality attention share}
{\footnotesize
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.15}
\begin{tabular}{l r r r}
\toprule
\textbf{Modality} & \textbf{Tokens} & \textbf{Total sal.} & \textbf{Per token} \\
\midrule
Video  & $\sim$3\,500 & 89.7\,\% & 1.0$\times$ (ref) \\
Audio  &   $\sim$200  &  6.2\,\% & \textbf{1.16}$\times$ \\
Text   &    $\sim$30  &  4.1\,\% & \textbf{5.1}$\times$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}}
\end{block}

\end{column}

\begin{column}{0.42\textwidth}

\begin{beamercolorbox}[sep=5pt,rounded=true,colsep*=0pt]{block body}
{\footnotesize
\textbf{The key fact.} Video has 17$\times$ more tokens than audio,
so its bulk saliency dominates. But \emph{per token}, audio and
text receive more attention than video. The model is not video-biased
by preference --- it is outvoted by token count alone.}
\end{beamercolorbox}

\vspace{6pt}

\begin{block}{Why this matters for the bottleneck}
{\footnotesize
The VIB compresses video tokens. Even modest compression (2.6$\times$,
$\mathrm{KL}_v: 12229\!\to\!4776$) brings the effective token budget
closer to audio. This should let audio tokens ``speak louder'' without
any explicit reweighting.}
\end{block}

\end{column}
\end{columns}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The VIB concentrates attention on the sound source}

\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.50\textwidth}

\begin{block}{Gradient saliency maps: without vs.\ with VIB}
{\footnotesize
Same checkpoint, same 6 clips. Left column: bottleneck disabled
(video tokens passed through). Right column: bottleneck enabled.
Maps show which video-token positions drive the final answer token.}
\end{block}

{\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{l r r}
\toprule
\textbf{Metric} & \textbf{No IB} & \textbf{With IB} \\
\midrule
Spatial entropy     & 0.78 & \textbf{0.69} ($\downarrow$11\,\%) \\
Top-5\% mass        & 0.53 & \textbf{0.74} ($\uparrow$40\,\%) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}}

\vspace{4pt}
{\footnotesize
\textbf{Spatial entropy}: $H=-\sum_i \bar s_i \log \bar s_i$ of the
normalised saliency map (lower = more localised).\\
\textbf{Top-5\% mass}: fraction of total saliency in the top 5\%
of positions (higher = more concentrated).}

\end{column}

\begin{column}{0.47\textwidth}

\begin{beamercolorbox}[sep=5pt,rounded=true,colsep*=0pt]{block body}
{\footnotesize
Without the bottleneck, saliency scatters over backgrounds and
irrelevant regions. With the bottleneck, it concentrates on the
sound source (the singer's face and microphone). The VIB acts as a
spatial filter that discards redundant background detail.}
\end{beamercolorbox}

\vspace{6pt}

\begin{block}{Connection to fail-safety}
{\footnotesize
Concentrated saliency is a necessary (not sufficient) condition for
fail-safety: if the model grounds its answer on the sound source,
zeroing that source should reliably collapse confidence. The DPO
abstention results support this: the model correctly abstains on
\texttt{vid\_zero} and \texttt{vid\_mean} --- exactly the conditions
where the grounding region is removed.}
\end{block}

\end{column}
\end{columns}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Audio attention: which segments drive the answer}

\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.55\textwidth}

\begin{block}{Method}
{\footnotesize
Same gradient saliency pass, restricted to audio token positions.
We map each audio token back to its time offset in the clip and
overlay the saliency curve on the waveform. Top-$k$ attended
time windows are marked.}
\end{block}

\begin{block}{Observations (30 AVHBench clips)}
{\footnotesize
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{2pt}
  \item The peak attended audio segment corresponds to the onset of the
        relevant sound event in $\sim$70\,\% of clips.
  \item Audio saliency is bursty: a 1--2\,s window typically accounts
        for $>$50\,\% of total audio saliency.
  \item The VIB model and the vanilla model show similar audio saliency
        patterns --- confirming that the VIB acts on video, not audio.
\end{itemize}}
\end{block}

\end{column}

\begin{column}{0.42\textwidth}

\begin{block}{What this rules out}
{\footnotesize
If audio saliency were uniform, the model would be integrating
the whole waveform regardless of content. The bursty pattern shows
the model has learned to attend to \emph{events} in the audio
stream, not just the presence/absence of sound.}
\end{block}

\begin{block}{Implication for the IB story}
{\footnotesize
The bottleneck compresses video, leaving audio untouched. The
audio saliency patterns are unchanged by the VIB, which is
consistent with the per-token attention analysis: audio tokens
were always attended to more carefully --- the VIB only removes
the numerical dominance of video bulk.}
\end{block}

\end{column}
\end{columns}

\end{frame}

\section{Open issues}

\begin{frame}{Two open issues from this week}

\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.50\textwidth}

\begin{block}{1.\ \texttt{vid\_noise} generalisation gap}
{\footnotesize
DPO abstains correctly on trained corruptions but not on
\texttt{vid\_noise} (white noise, held-out). Two possible fixes:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{2pt}
  \item \textbf{Add noise to the training set.} Simple, but we'd
        need to enumerate all ``confusable'' corruptions.
  \item \textbf{Train on the VIB's $\sigma$ signal.} When the
        bottleneck sees high-entropy input, $\sigma$ is high.
        If the model learns ``$\sigma_{\max}\!\uparrow\Rightarrow$
        abstain,'' it generalises across any corruption that raises
        the VIB's posterior uncertainty --- including noise.
  \end{enumerate}}
\end{block}

\end{column}

\begin{column}{0.47\textwidth}

\begin{block}{2.\ Risk metric is degenerate (evaluation bug)}
{\footnotesize
All conditions report risk\,=\,1.0 (zero correct answers when the
model does answer). Root cause: the answer-extraction regex expects
``yes''/``no'' directly, but the DPO model now sometimes says
``yes, \ldots'' or ``no, because\ldots''. The coverage numbers are
valid (they count abstention tokens exactly); the risk numbers need
a fuzzy match fix before they can be reported.}
\end{block}

\begin{block}{Fix plan}
{\footnotesize
Update \texttt{eval/risk\_coverage.py}: first token after the
answer marker determines yes/no/cant; discard the rest of the
generation when computing accuracy. Re-run eval on existing
checkpoints (no re-training needed).}
\end{block}

\end{column}
\end{columns}

\end{frame}

\section{Next steps}

\begin{frame}{Next steps}

\begin{block}{1.\ Fix the risk metric and re-read the 6-way table}
  Patch the answer extractor (first-token rule). Re-run evaluation on
  all saved checkpoints. The coverage table already shows DPO works;
  the risk table will tell us whether DPO preserved task accuracy.
  This is the primary deliverable for next week.
\end{block}

\begin{block}{2.\ Extend DPO to \texttt{vid\_noise}}
  Add Gaussian noise corruptions to the preference-pair mining step.
  Re-run DPO. Target: coverage on \texttt{vid\_noise} drops from
  94\,\% to $<$20\,\% while clean coverage stays $>$95\,\%.
\end{block}

\begin{block}{3.\ Test the $\sigma$-signal hypothesis}
  Log $\max_j\sigma_j$ from the VIB on the same 150-item eval set
  across all conditions. If $\sigma$ rises reliably on corrupted
  inputs, it provides a corruption-agnostic abstention feature ---
  and the strongest argument for keeping the bottleneck.
\end{block}

\begin{block}{4.\ Transfer the winning model to AVHBench hallucination task}
  Once the risk metric is fixed and DPO generalises to noise,
  re-run the Video-driven Audio Hallucination subset. Target: erase
  the $-8.3$\,pp regression from the last report.
\end{block}
